Gaze the Haze

See how hazy Singapore's air is today, hour by hour and region by region, and compare it with every year since 2014.

The presence of haze in Singapore has transitioned from an unfamiliar weather oddity into a documented environmental crisis. Before the Pollutant Standards Index (PSI) was established in 1991, the country relied on news archives to note haze tracking.

▼

Each day shows its lowest - highest reading

Tap a region on the map to highlight its row. Tap an hour to see each region at that time.

Guidance is loosely modelled on NEA's PSI bands. Check nea.gov.sg for official advice.

Compare the past

Each circle is a year. Small and light was clean, big and dark was hazy. The big number inside is the year's peak, its highest single-day reading, with the year's lowest in small type above it. Under each circle is how many hazy days that year had, then its twelve months from January (top) to December (bottom). Tap a circle or a month for details.

N marks a haze event recorded in newspapers before readings existed. Readings before 2014 did not include PM2.5, so they cannot be compared like-for-like.

Why it happened

Pick causes to show only the months where they applied. A marker shows one cause per month; hover a month for all of them. Tags are placeholders inferred from season and level, not verified attributions.

Hover or tap a cause to see what it means and why it happens.

Data Source

National Environment Agency (NEA) via data.gov.sg, under the Singapore Open Data Licence. Each day shows the highest regional 24-hour PSI reading recorded that day, with the lowest in small type above it. The dataset may contain missing records and has not been through NEA's climate-data quality control; for official records contact sales_climo@nea.gov.sg.
